    Step 9 · Other designs
    
 
    Build it another way
    
 
    The book lifts the weight with a cord and a pulley. Engineers always ask: is there another way? Look at two other designs in 3D, then invent your own. There is no single right answer: use your kit, your ideas and your team.
    
 
    Book design
    Cord over a pulley
    The servo winds the cord on a spool. The pulley turns the pull around so the weight goes straight up.
    
 
    Example 1
    Lever arm (like a biceps curl)
    
  
    No cord and no pulley. A long Technic beam is fixed to the servo horn. When the servo turns, the beam swings up like your forearm, and the weight hangs from its end. This is a lever: the servo is the pivot.
    
  
    
  
    ✔ GoodVery easy to build: one beam, one axle. It looks just like the Joints project!
    ✘ Watch outA long arm needs a lot of turning force. Use a light weight or a shorter beam.
    
  
    Parts: 270° servo, one long Technic beam, axle, cord or hook, small weight.
    
 
    Example 2
    Weight stack with guide rails (like a real gym)
    
  
    The book design, plus two straight axles standing up on both sides of the weight. The weight slides up and down between them, so it cannot swing or spin. Real gym machines work this way!
    
  
    
  
    ✔ GoodMuch safer: the weight always moves in a straight line, even if the user pulls quickly.
    ✘ Watch outThe rails must be straight and smooth, or the weight gets stuck.
    
  
    Parts: everything from the book design + 2 long axles, 2 bricks to hold them, a beam with holes on the weight.

    Tips and hints
    
  
      
   
    • Small spool = less force, slower lift. A big spool lifts faster but the servo works harder.
      • 
   
    • More pulleys = less force, but you need more cord and the weight moves less.
      • 
   
    • Make it strong: add cross beams in a triangle shape. Triangles do not bend.
      • 
   
    • Make it safe: keep fingers away from the cord, move slowly, and never go past 180° in your code.
      • 
   
    • Show useful things on the OLED: reps, time, or a smiley face when the set is done.
      • 
   
    • Add a sensor: a button to start, or an ultrasonic sensor that stops the machine if a hand comes too close.

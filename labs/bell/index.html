    Step 8 · Other designs

    Other doorbells with the same kit

    Every design below uses only parts from your Early Electronics Kit. Press a design to hear and see it at the door.

    Book design

    Button doorbell with music

    Button 14 + Musical IC 21 + Loudspeaker 20: a tune plays when you press.

    Design 1

    Touch-plate doorbell

    Use the touch plate instead of the button: the visitor just touches it with a finger.

    ✔ GoodNo moving parts to break.
    ✘ Watch outGloves do not work on it.
    Design 2

    Buzzer doorbell

    Button + piezo buzzer: a simple "bzzz" with no musical IC.

    ✔ GoodVery simple: only 3 parts and wires.
    ✘ Watch outNo tune, just one sound.
    Design 3

    Shop-door chime

    Reed switch on the door frame and a magnet on the door: it plays when the door opens.

    ✔ GoodTells you someone came in.
    ✘ Watch outNot manually activated, so it does not meet this project's rule.
    Design 4

    Light doorbell

    Add a red LED: it lights up when the tune plays, so people who cannot hear well can see it.

    ✔ GoodHelps everyone in the family.
    ✘ Watch outYou must be looking at it.
    Design 5

    Shadow doorbell

    A photoresistor by the door: when a visitor's shadow covers it, the bell rings.

    ✔ GoodRings before the visitor even presses.
    ✘ Watch outA cat or a cloud can ring it too!

    Tips and hints

    • Every circuit needs a complete loop: battery → switch → parts → back to the battery.
    • Something must start it (button, touch plate…) and something must make the sound (speaker or buzzer).
    • Never connect the battery's two ends with only a wire: that is a short circuit.
    • Test one change at a time, then ring it again.
    • Think about the people who will use it: children, visitors, someone who cannot hear well.

    Step 9 · Other designs
    
 
    Build it another way
    
 
    The book asks you to improve the example. Here are five other ways to build or model chromatography, in 3D. Then invent your own.
    
 
    Book design
    Paper strip + OLED lines
    A strip hangs in a cup; the OLED shows lines.
    
 
    Example 1
    Servo dipper
    A servo lowers the strip into the water at exactly the right depth, and lifts it out after a set time. No more strips falling in!
    
  
    ✔ GoodEvery strip gets the same depth and time: a fair test.
    ✘ Watch outKeep the controller far from the water.
    
 
    Example 2
    Circular chromatography (coffee filter)
    Put the ink dot in the middle of a coffee filter and feed water to the centre with a paper wick. The colours spread out in rings.
    
  
    ✔ GoodBeautiful rings, easy to compare colours.
    ✘ Watch outHarder to measure distances: measure from the centre.
    
 
    Example 3
    LED water-front meter
    A column of LEDs next to the strip lights up as the water climbs, so everyone can see the front from far away.
    
  
    ✔ GoodShows the solvent front clearly in class.
    ✘ Watch outYou must time it: the LEDs follow your code, not the real water.
    
 
    Example 4
    Colour scanner
    A servo slides a colour sensor up beside the strip and draws a graph of what it sees on the OLED: one peak for each band, like a real lab machine.
    
  
    ✔ GoodThis is how real labs read chromatograms.
    ✘ Watch outThe sensor must move slowly and stay close to the paper.
    
 
    Example 5
    OLED timer + buzzer
    The controller counts the minutes on the OLED and beeps when it is time to take the strip out, before the water reaches the top.
    
  
    ✔ GoodYou never miss the right moment.
    ✘ Watch outDifferent papers are faster or slower: test the time first.

    Tips and hints
    
  
      
   
    • Always draw the start line in pencil. Pencil (graphite) does not dissolve, so it does not run.
      • 
   
    • Keep the dot above the water. If the dot is under water, the ink washes into the cup.
      • 
   
    • Small, dark dots work best. Go over the dot 3–4 times, letting it dry in between.
      • 
   
    • Cover the cup so the water does not evaporate from the paper.
      • 
   
    • Rubbing alcohol is flammable: only with your teacher, away from flames.

    🎉 Project complete: Reflection
    
  
    You bounced an invisible infrared signal off a mirror and proved the law of reflection: the angle of incidence equals the angle of reflection. The same idea carries signals in security systems, fibre-optic internet and TV remotes (SDG 9: industry, innovation and infrastructure).

    Step 10 · Other designs
    
 
    Build it another way
    
 
    The same remote, receiver and mirrors can do many jobs. Run each design in 3D and watch the beam (shown as if through a phone camera), then invent your own.
    
 
    Book design
    One mirror
    The signal bounces once, off a mirror on a stand, to the receiver.
    
 
    Example 1
    Periscope
    Two mirrors at 45° send the signal over a tall wall, like a submarine periscope.
    
  
    ✔ GoodReaches a receiver you cannot see.
    ✘ Watch outBoth mirrors must be exactly 45°, and parallel.
    
 
    Example 2
    Corner reflector
    Two mirrors at 90° send the signal straight back to where it came from, from almost any angle. Bike reflectors and road signs use this.
    
  
    ✔ GoodNo careful aiming needed.
    ✘ Watch outPut the receiver right beside the remote.
    
 
    Example 3
    Doorway alarm
    The beam zig-zags across a doorway between mirrors. When someone walks through, the signal stops and the alarm sounds.
    
  
    ✔ GoodOne beam guards a whole doorway.
    ✘ Watch outNeeds an IR LED that sends all the time, not a button.
    
 
    Example 4
    Self-aiming mirror
    A servo on port 13 slowly turns the mirror. As soon as the receiver gets the code, the servo stops: the mirror has aimed itself.
    
  
    ✔ GoodFinds the angle by itself.
    ✘ Watch outTurn slowly, or it will pass the right angle.
    
 
    Example 5
    Around the box
    Three mirrors carry the signal all the way around a box, like signals routed around a building.
    
  
    ✔ GoodShows how a signal can be routed anywhere.
    ✘ Watch outEvery bounce loses some signal: keep the path short.

    Tips and hints
    
  
      
   
    • Measure from the normal: the line at 90° to the mirror. Both angles are measured from it.
      • 
   
    • See the invisible: look at the remote's LED through a phone camera to check it is sending.
      • 
   
    • Steady the mirror: the curved blue beams stop it from wobbling. A tiny tilt moves the beam a lot far away.
      • 
   
    • Keep it short: every bounce loses some signal, and the remote's range is only a few metres.
      • 
   
    • Use the mirror-image trick to plan where to aim before you build.

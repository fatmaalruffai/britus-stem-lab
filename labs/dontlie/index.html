Step 1 · Problem and plan

Can a circuit tell if someone is lying?

The problem (book)

A lie detector (polygraph) records the body's reactions while a person answers questions: blood pressure, pulse and respiration. When people lie they often feel nervous, and the body changes.

Challenge (book): Build the Lie Detector with an Indicator from the Circuit Kit. Its galvanometer (indicator) shows a change when a finger touches the touch plate.

Vocabulary (book)

Brainstorm: a nervous person's hands often get a little sweaty. What does sweat (salty water) do?
Step 2 · Build the circuit (book)

Snap the parts together

Book parts: 2 wire with 2 contacts (×3) · 5 wire with 5 contacts · 14 button · 15 switch · 19 battery compartment · 33 resistor 10K (×2) · 51 transistor PNP · 55 galvanometer. Then do book step 1: replace the button 14 with the touch plate 12. Watch out for trap parts! Mistakes are counted.

    Step 3 · Test it (book)

    Touch the plate

    Book: 1) Replace the button 14 with the touch plate 12. 2) Turn on the switch 15. 3) When a finger is put on the touch plate 12, the microammeter reacts to a change.

    1. Turn on switch 15.
    2. Put a dry finger on the touch plate.
    3. Now try a sweaty finger (a nervous person).
    4. Take your finger away.
    Reading the meter: each number = 50 µA (microamperes).
    Why does the needle move further with a sweaty finger?

Guidance: everything you need for this project

Open any section when you are stuck. You can come back here at any time.

Goals and rules
  • Combine substances using the DC motor.
  • Speed up and slow down by turning the potentiometer.
  • Use only the materials provided, and include design ideas from every team member.
Team roles
  • Leader: keeps the team on task.
  • Material Manager: chooses and tracks the parts.
  • Reporter: explains the design to the class.
  • Testing Supervisor: runs the tests and records results.
Parts: needed and not needed

Needed: Uno board (smart kit), USB cable, DC motor, potentiometer, Lego bricks and beams, wires.
Not needed here: ultrasonic, buzzer, servo, joystick, LED, IR receiver, remote.

Build tips with Lego
  1. Press the base plate flat so the studs face up.
  2. Build two matching pillars of the same height, holes facing inward.
  3. Join the top beam so the frame is square and does not wobble.
  4. Add the green cross bar in the middle of the frame. It holds both axles.
  5. Mount the DC motor high on the left upright, with its shaft pointing toward the axles.
  6. Hang two vertical axles through the cross bar.
  7. Add the small motor gear and the two axle gears so they touch and mesh.
  8. Fix a set of blades on each axle, low enough to reach the sand and salt but above the cup bottom.

How the program works
  1. The potentiometer sends an analog value from 0 to 1023.
  2. The controller divides it by 4 to get a PWM signal from 0 to 255.
  3. PWM tells the motor how fast to turn: 0 is stopped, 255 is full speed.
Science and vocabulary
  • Moment (torque): turning effect of a force around a pivot. Moment = force × distance.
  • Newton’s second law: F = m × a.
  • Resistance: a force that opposes motion or electrical current.
  • Analog: a signal that changes continuously.
  • Volume of a cylinder: π × r² × h.
One motor, two mixers: how the gears work
  • The small gear on the motor turns the first axle gear.
  • The first axle gear meshes with the second, so the two axles turn in opposite directions.
  • Gears that do not touch will not turn, and gears pressed too hard will jam.
  • Bigger gears turn slower but give more moment (turning force).
Troubleshooting
  • Motor does not spin: check the wires, then check that the program is uploaded.
  • Motor only spins at high settings: the blades may meet too much resistance. Use less material or shorter blades.
  • Blades wobble: make the shaft straight and the frame square.
  • Nothing changes when you turn the dial: check that it is in Port 2 and the code reads Port 2.
Why it matters

Better-designed machines mix materials efficiently, which reduces waste and saves energy. Mechanical engineers design machines like this for factories.

Team build challenges
  1. Same direction: change the gear train so both mixers spin the same way. Explain why it works.
  2. Speed limit: change the program so the motor never goes above PWM 200. Hint: use an If block.
  3. Fair test: time how long the cup takes to mix at PWM 100 and at PWM 250. Record both in a table. Which is better, and why?
  4. Blade design: try short blades, then long blades. Which mixes faster? Which needs more moment from the motor?
  5. Third mixer: sketch a design where one motor drives three mixers. Which way does each one turn?

8. Other ways to build a mixer

The book shows one design: a small motor gear turning two axle gears. Engineers always ask: is there another way? Study two new designs, then invent your own.

Example 1

Belt and pulleys

The motor sits in the middle of the top beam. A rubber band (the belt) wraps around a small pulley on the motor and a big pulley on each mixer axle. When the motor turns, the belt pulls both pulleys round.

✔ GoodBoth mixers turn the same direction. Quiet, and the axles can be far apart.
✘ Watch outA loose belt slips in thick sand. Keep the rubber band tight.

Parts: DC motor, 1 small pulley, 2 big pulleys, 1 rubber band, 2 axles, 2 blades. Ratio: big pulley ÷ small pulley ≈ 2.25, so blades turn 2.25× slower with 2.25× more moment.

Example 2

Planetary mixer (like a kitchen stand mixer)

Seen from above. The motor slowly turns an arm. Each end of the arm carries a beater that also spins fast on its own axle. The beaters travel all around the bowl, so no corner is left unmixed. The dotted line shows the path of one blade tip.

✔ GoodMixes the whole bowl, including the edges. Best for sand and salt that settle at the sides.
✘ Watch outMore parts and harder to build. The arm must be balanced or the frame shakes.

Parts: DC motor pointing down from the top beam, 1 centre gear, 1 arm (a beam), 2 small gears with axles, 2 blades. The small gears roll around the fixed centre gear, which makes the beaters spin.

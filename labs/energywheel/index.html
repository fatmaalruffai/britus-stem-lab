    Step 10 · Other designs
    
 
    Build it another way
    
 
    The book builds a Ferris wheel. Is there another way to show an energy cycle? Look at five other designs in 3D, then invent your own. There is no single right answer.
    
 
    Book design
    Kit Ferris wheel
    A chain of gears carries the servo's turn up to the wheel. The bins hang from the ends and stay upright while the wheel turns.
    
 
    Example 1
    Turntable (like a lazy Susan)
    
  
    Lay the wheel flat. A big gear plate sits right on top of the servo, and the bins stand on it in a circle. A pointer marks the level you are talking about.
    
  
    
  
    ✔ GoodVery stable and easy to build. Everyone around the table can see it.
    ✘ Watch outIt is flat, so it is harder to show the sun shining down on the producers.
    
  
    Parts: 360° servo, big gear or round plate, 4 small bins, a pointer beam, base plate.
    
 
    Example 2
    Direct drive (no gear chain)
    
  
    The kit model carries the turn up a chain of 5 gears. Instead, fix the servo high up behind the wheel and put its shaft straight onto the axle. No gears needed!
    
  
    
  
    ✔ GoodFewer parts and quicker to build. Nothing can slip.
    ✘ Watch outThe servo sits high up, so it needs a strong holder, and its cable must reach far.
    
  
    Parts: kit model without the short axles, the 5 gears and the motor stand + a tall holder for the servo.
    
 
    Example 3
    Energy pyramid carousel
    
  
    Stack four round plates, from big to small, on one axle: producers at the bottom, carnivores at the top. The servo turns the whole tower. The shrinking plates show that less energy reaches each level.
    
  
    
  
    ✔ GoodIt shows the 10% rule with its shape: the higher the level, the smaller the plate.
    ✘ Watch outA tall tower can wobble. Keep the axle straight and the top plates light.
    
  
    Parts: 360° servo, 4 round plates or gears of different sizes, a long axle, bushings, base plate.
    
 
    Example 4
    Conveyor loop (like the kit's Conveyor)
    
  
    Two wheels and a belt make a loop on the table. The bins ride on the belt one after the other, so the levels always pass in the right order and come back again.
    
  
    
  
    ✔ GoodIt looks like a real cycle: matter keeps moving round and round. You can add as many bins as you like.
    ✘ Watch outThe belt must be tight, or it slips off the wheels.
    
  
    Parts: 360° servo, 2 big gears or wheels, a rubber band or chain as the belt, beams for the frame (see the kit's Project 07 Conveyor).
    
 
    Example 5
    Pointer dial (like a clock)
    
  
    Draw the energy cycle on a round board. The servo turns only a pointer, like the hand of a clock, from one level to the next.
    
  
    
  
    ✔ GoodThe easiest and lightest build. The servo hardly has to work.
    ✘ Watch outIt is flat: there are no bins to fill with animals or poison dots.
    
  
    Parts: 360° servo, one beam as the pointer, a round card or plate with your drawing, two beams to stand it up.

    Tips and hints
    
  
      
   
    • Balance the wheel: put bins of the same weight opposite each other, or the servo struggles on one side.
      • 
   
    • Slow is better for teaching: one turn in 8 to 15 seconds gives time to explain each level.
      • 
   
    • Use local animals: a desert wheel could be: desert plants → camel or gazelle → Arabian wolf → fungi and bacteria.
      • 
   
    • Show the poison: stick purple dots on the bins, more at each level, to show bioaccumulation.
      • 
   
    • Use the OLED: show the name of the level at the top, or the energy left (10,000 → 1,000 → 100 kJ).
      • 
   
    • Two buttons: one to start and one to pause, or a potentiometer for speed.

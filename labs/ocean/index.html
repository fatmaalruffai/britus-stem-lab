    Step 9 · Other designs

    Build it another way

    Engineers around the world are trying many ideas to stop plastic and take it out of the water. Run each idea in 3D, then design your own.

    Book designs

    Reel, Sweep and Grab

    Your three machines working together on the bay.

    Example 1

    Floating barrier

    A long U-shaped floating barrier lets the current carry plastic into it. No motor is needed to move the plastic.

    ✔ GoodUses the moving water for free.
    ✘ Watch outMust not trap fish; boats empty it.
    Example 2

    Skimmer boat

    A boat drives slowly and a conveyor belt at the front lifts floating plastic into a basket.

    ✔ GoodGoes to where the plastic is.
    ✘ Watch outNeeds two motors: one to drive, one for the belt.
    Example 3

    Trash wheel

    The river current turns a water wheel. The wheel drives a conveyor that lifts plastic out of the river before it reaches the sea.

    ✔ GoodPowered by the river itself.
    ✘ Watch outOnly works where the water flows.
    Example 4

    River-mouth net

    A net across the end of a river catches plastic before it reaches the ocean.

    ✔ GoodStops plastic early.
    ✘ Watch outMust be emptied often, and boats must get past.
    Example 5

    Robot boat with Grab

    A small robot boat drives to each bottle and picks it up with a grabber.

    ✔ GoodPicks only plastic, not fish.
    ✘ Watch outSlow: one piece at a time.

    Tips and hints

    • Match the catcher to the plastic: nets for bottles and bags, sweepers or fine screens for small pieces, grabbers for one big piece.
    • Change one thing at a time when you test, like you did in Discover.
    • Use free energy: currents, rivers and wind can move plastic for you.
    • Think about animals: slow machines and the right hole size let fish escape.
    • Stop it at the start: catching plastic in rivers is easier than in the open sea, and using less plastic is best of all.

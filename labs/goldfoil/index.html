    Step 4 · Design the gold foil (book build steps 2–3)
    
 
    Cut the holes in your "gold foil"
    
 
    Your red card is an atom. Holes are empty space. Solid parts are the nucleus (protons and neutrons). Tap a circle to cut it out, or tap it again to fill it. Your atom must follow Rutherford's rules below.
    
 
    
 
    1. Most of the atom is empty space (at least 11 of 15 holes).
    2. The nucleus is in the centre of the atom.
    3. The nucleus is one small clump (solid parts touch each other).

    Step 10 · Other designs
    
 
    Build it another way
    
 
    The book asks: will you follow the image exactly, or innovate? Look at five other ways to model the experiment, in 3D, then invent your own.
    
 
    Book design
    Conveyor belt + card foil
    A 360° servo drives the belt, which carries the particles to the red card.
    
 
    Example 1
    Marble ramp (no motor)
    
  
    Roll marbles down a sloping beam. Gravity is the "accelerator". Change the starting height to change the speed.
    
  
    
  
    ✔ GoodVery cheap and quick, no electronics. Speed is easy to change.
    ✘ Watch outMarbles do not always roll straight. Add rails on the sides.
    
 
    Example 2
    Magnet nucleus
    
  
    Real alpha particles are pushed, not blocked. Put a strong magnet in the middle and small magnets (same pole facing) on the particles. They curve away before they even touch!
    
  
    
  
    ✔ GoodClosest to the real science: repulsion between positive charges.
    ✘ Watch outStrong magnets can pinch fingers and wipe cards and phones. Ask your teacher.
    
 
    Example 3
    Mystery box (hidden nucleus)
    
  
    Rutherford never saw the nucleus. Hide a shape inside a box, fire particles and mark where they bounce. Can your classmates guess the shape?
    
  
    
  
    ✔ GoodIt shows how scientists find things they cannot see.
    ✘ Watch outYou need many particles to get a clear picture.
    
 
    Example 4
    Detector ring
    
  
    In the real experiment, a glowing screen all around the foil showed where each particle went. Add a half-ring of paper to mark the angle of every bounce.
    
  
    
  
    ✔ GoodYou can measure the angles, not just pass or bounce.
    ✘ Watch outThe ring must be the same distance from the foil all the way round.
    
 
    Example 5
    Servo flicker
    
  
    No belt: a 270° servo arm flicks each particle towards the foil, like a tiny tennis racket. You set the angle and speed in your code.
    
  
    
  
    ✔ GoodFast particles, and you can aim them with code.
    ✘ Watch outToo fast and the particles fly off the table. Start slow.

    Tips and hints
    
  
      
   
    • More particles = better results. 10 particles can fool you; 100 give a clear pattern.
      • 
   
    • Make the nucleus tiny. The emptier your foil, the closer you get to a real atom.
      • 
   
    • Use the ultrasonic sensor as a counter: count every particle that passes, and show it on the OLED.
      • 
   
    • Slow motion helps: a slow belt lets the class see each particle hit or pass.
      • 
   
    • Mark the results: put paper behind the foil and mark where each particle lands.
